About Me

Agile leadership with a human heart

I believe the best technology is built by teams who trust each other — not by processes and tools alone. Over 13 years across industries, I've brought that philosophy to life as a Scrum Master and Virtual Product Owner in the healthcare interoperability domain.

Today, I lead 2 agile development teams — part of a career spanning 8–10 agile teams led across industries — working on products that directly affect how healthcare providers submit, retrieve, and manage patient records. The stakes are real: every workflow we improve means faster care, fewer errors, and less administrative burden on clinical staff.

My approach is simple: remove friction, protect the team's focus, translate complex business needs into clear deliverables, and celebrate every inch of progress. My non-technical background is a genuine strength — I ask the questions that engineers forget to ask, and I connect technical work to human outcomes in a way that keeps everyone aligned.

People First
Teams perform best when they feel safe, heard, and valued. I invest in individuals before investing in process.
Clarity Over Complexity

Continuous Improvement

My Approach

How I coach teams

"A great Scrum Master doesn't manage work — they manage the conditions for great work."

01
Build psychological safety first
Teams that feel safe to flag blockers, admit mistakes, and ask "dumb questions" outperform those who don't. I create that environment through consistent 1:1s, transparent retrospectives, and modelling vulnerability myself.
02
Connect the "what" to the "why"
Engineers are far more motivated when they understand the human problem behind a user story. I ensure every story has a clear business context — not just acceptance criteria — so the team builds with intent, not just compliance.
03
Make the invisible visible
Impediments, risks, and dependencies kill velocity when they hide. I surface these early, create visibility through structured dashboards, and bring the right people into the conversation before small issues become big ones.
04
Serve the team, don't manage it
My role is to multiply the team's capacity — not to track their tasks. I shield the team from organisational noise, advocate for their needs upward, and step back enough to let self-organisation emerge naturally.
